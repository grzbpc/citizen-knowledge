### The story

A cyclist who reported a motorist for bad driving was himself taken to court after his helmet camera footage exposed his own careless riding. He had reported what he described as a dangerous manoeuvre, but the clip showed that he had taken risks himself shortly afterwards. He was charged with riding without due care and attention and fined £1,200 by Bradford magistrates.

### The reframe

The riding happened whether or not he reported anything. What did not exist until he made the complaint was a record of it in a form the system could act on. He was not caught. He filed himself.

This is the mechanism the book calls threshold logic, running in its purest form. The offence was real, the law was in force, and the enforcement did not follow from the conduct. It followed from visibility, and the visibility was self-generated. Every other cyclist and driver taking comparable risks on that road that afternoon remains unprosecuted, not because their behaviour was different but because no camera on their head produced a file for anybody to open.

The practical lesson circulating in the comments under stories like this one will be that helmet cameras are a liability. The structural lesson is less comfortable. In a system where enforcement depends on legibility rather than on conduct, the rational move is to stay unrecorded, which means the rational move is not to report anything. Whatever the fine was intended to teach, that is what it teaches.
