### The story

Robert Bush, 48, is facing sentencing at Hull Crown Court over horrors discovered at his Legacy Independent Funeral Directors business, where 31 bodies, including a stillborn baby, were left to rot while grieving families were falsely told cremations had taken place. Daughter Tina Thames said: "Dad told me and my brother and sister we didn't want a fancy or expensive funeral, he just wanted us to celebrate his life." Jim Porter's family, whose body was recovered from the parlour with hospital identification bands still on his wrists and ankle, noticed at his funeral that his coffin did not look heavy enough for him. Angela Scottow's family contacted her for a plot near her sister's, after being told her sister's ashes were in a Christmas Eve casket, only to learn there was no record of her being cremated there. Some of Bush's victims have held back their statements as they read out in court.

### The reframe

Sixty-seven offences, 31 bodies, years of families being told cremations had happened when they had not: what stands out is not that one funeral director deceived people on this scale, but that nothing in the regulatory structure around funeral homes caught it while it was happening. Families noticed discrepancies themselves, a coffin that felt too light, ashes that could not be traced, hospital identification bands still attached, and pieced the truth together largely on their own. The industry that handles the dead operates, on this evidence, largely on trust rather than routine verification, and trust of that kind fails silently until someone outside the system happens to notice.
