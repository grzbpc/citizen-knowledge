### The story

A gang crime expert has warned that releasing prisoners early, including those serving sentences for gang-related offending, risks inadvertently raising their status within gang hierarchies. Describing how the incentive works, he explained: "If you are a drug dealer for a gang, go to prison and don't snitch on any higher-ups, it shows to them you are trustworthy. Being released from prison is one of the ways you progress. You walk out with a higher status." Andy Burnham has said he wanted to go further than the government's current carve-outs in excluding serious offenders from the scheme.

### The reframe

Most criticism of an early release scheme argues that it is too lenient in the abstract: it lets people out sooner than a sentence implies, and that feels wrong on its face. This is a different and more precise objection. It is not that the scheme is lenient. It is that, inside a specific structure, gang hierarchy, early release functions as a promotion.

The manuscript's account of what happens when enforcement systems measure their own success only in terms legible to the institution running them applies directly here:

> *Success reads upward, not outward. [...] The system optimises for defensibility, not resolution.*

An early release scheme can be entirely defensible in its own terms: prison capacity managed, statutory tests met, categories of exclusion published and expanded when challenged. None of that requires anyone inside the system to ask what early release actually communicates to the population it is releasing, in the specific social structure that population belongs to. The gang expert quoted here is not making an abstract point about leniency. He is describing a mechanism: silence plus a shortened sentence equals status. That mechanism does not appear anywhere in how the scheme measures its own success, because the scheme's metrics were never built to see it.

This is the gap the manuscript keeps returning to: the difference between a system that can defend its own operation in its own terms, and a system that has actually accounted for what its operation does once it leaves the institution and enters the world it releases people back into.
