### The story

Uefa's leadership has told Fifa president Gianni Infantino that his response to football's ongoing integrity crisis amounts to "nothing short of changed", rejecting his reassurances as insufficient with the World Cup approaching. The unease spans several fronts: how the game is governed at grassroots level, whether officials can be trusted, and whether Fifa's own decision making withstands scrutiny. Infantino has held his position through previous rounds of criticism, and Wednesday's intervention makes clear the spotlight remains on him rather than on any specific reform already delivered.

### The reframe

What is notable about this story is not the criticism itself, football politics runs on criticism, but the shape of Fifa's response to it. Reassurance has been offered. Concern has been acknowledged. Confidence has been expressed that the institution is capable of addressing the issues raised. None of that is the same as a change that can be pointed to and checked.

The manuscript describes this substitution as a structural feature of institutions under pressure, not an occasional lapse:

> *A railway line is announced years before construction begins. Housing targets are published long before homes exist. Crime strategies are launched before the institutions responsible for implementing them possess the resources required to do so. The political rewards arrive immediately. The practical consequences arrive later, if they arrive at all.*

Fifa's version of this is reassurance rather than announcement, but the mechanism is identical. A statement of concern is issued. It functions, for as long as no one asks what specifically has changed, as evidence that the concern is being addressed. Uefa's rejection of that reassurance as insufficient is itself notable, because it names the gap directly rather than accepting the statement as a substitute for its content. Whether that naming produces anything more concrete before the World Cup begins is the actual test. Everything before that point is, on the manuscript's terms, still just language filling space where delivery has not yet arrived.
