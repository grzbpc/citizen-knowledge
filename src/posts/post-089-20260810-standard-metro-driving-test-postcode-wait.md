### The story

Half of learner drivers are now waiting more than four months for a driving test at sixty centres across Britain, figures show. The median wait between booking and taking a test in June was 17.3 weeks, including at major sites such as Birmingham, Edinburgh, Glasgow and London. Forfar, in Angus, had the longest wait of all, at 29.7 weeks. Steve Gooding of the RAC called the delay frustrating and said learners should practise while they wait.

### The reframe

Seventeen point three weeks is the figure that will end up in the summary. It is the number a minister could cite, the one a headline can carry, the one that sounds, on its own, like a manageable and improving system under pressure rather than one that has quietly stopped working in specific places.

A learner in Forfar is not waiting 17.3 weeks. They are waiting 29.7, nearly double the median that describes the system they are supposedly part of. The national figure is accurate as a summary. It is not a description of what booking a test in Angus actually involves.

The manuscript's account of how institutions manage the gap between a headline metric and the underlying condition it claims to represent describes exactly this kind of split:

<blockquote class="quote--manuscript"><p>The number improves. The problem remains.</p></blockquote>

A median works by absorbing outliers into an average that no longer resembles any single person's experience. The centres coping reasonably well pull the figure down. The centres failing badly get folded into the same number, present in the calculation but invisible in the outcome. Nobody responsible for the national wait time is lying when they quote 17.3 weeks. But the figure functions less as a description of the driving test system than as a description of the driving test system in aggregate, a distinction that matters enormously to the person who lives in the place the aggregate is hiding.

This is not a story about driving tests specifically. It is a story about what happens whenever a service is measured nationally but delivered locally, and the number chosen to represent it is the one least likely to reflect where the service is actually failing. The learner in Forfar does not experience the median. They experience the wait. And the wait, this month, is nearly double what the country is being told to expect.
