### The story

Mohammad Alturk, 39, a Jordanian asylum seeker who fled police in an uninsured BMW, was caught with eight wraps of cocaine, and had been stopped with drugs weeks earlier without being charged, was given a community resolution rather than prosecution at that time, Basildon court heard. He admitted possession, explaining he had used cocaine to cope with stress but had since stopped. He was fined £120 and ordered to pay a £48 court surcharge. Outside court he smiled, gave a thumbs up, and declared: "All the best for all British! Love!" His asylum application is pending.

### The reframe

Two stops, weeks apart, for what the court heard was the same underlying behaviour, produced two different official responses: a community resolution the first time, a fine and surcharge the second. Neither outcome was automatic. Each was a decision made by someone with discretion to make it, presented afterwards as simply what the process required.
