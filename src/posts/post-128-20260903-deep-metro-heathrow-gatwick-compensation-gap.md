### The story

Villagers living under Heathrow's flight path say a compensation offer of £3,000 linked to plans for a third runway is unfair, after residents near Gatwick were offered £6,500 for a proposed second runway. People in Stanwell said the mooted sum falls short of the £6,500 offered to residents near Gatwick, which is set to build a second runway. The Heathrow expansion "nightmare" was updated in June.

### The reframe, with the manuscript

Two sets of residents, both living under the flight path of a planned runway expansion, both accepting the same basic bargain, namely that a major aviation project will disrupt their lives in exchange for money, have been handed two different prices for identical disruption. One group gets £6,500. The other gets £3,000. Nobody has argued that Stanwell's noise, disruption or loss of amenity is worth less than Gatwick's. The figure was simply arrived at somewhere else, by someone else, using criteria the residents themselves were never shown.

This is threshold logic operating exactly as the manuscript describes it, not as an aberration but as the system's normal mode:

> *Threshold enforcement distorts legitimacy. Because punishment is rare but severe, it acquires symbolic weight. Each instance stands in for many unaddressed ones. Control is signalled not through consistency, but through occasional demonstration.*

Compensation is not punishment, but the mechanism is the same one inverted. A single, visible figure, £3,000 here, £6,500 there, stands in for a negotiation the public never sees. Nobody at Heathrow has to explain why the number is what it is. It simply arrives, attached to a scheme description and a consultation deadline, as though it were the only number that could have resulted. The absence of a stated formula is not incidental. It is what allows the figure to move.

What the Stanwell residents are experiencing is the shift the manuscript identifies at the level of behaviour, not just policy:

> *The question shifts from "Am I within the rule?" to "Am I close enough to attract attention?"*

Nobody living near a runway gets to ask whether their disruption meets some fixed, published bar for compensation, because no such bar exists in public. What they can do is compare themselves to Gatwick and notice the gap. That comparison is the only enforcement mechanism available to them, and it arrives after the offer has already been made, not before it was calculated.

The frustration in Stanwell is not really about the number £3,000 in isolation. It is about what the number implies once a second number exists to measure it against. A resident who never learned that Gatwick's offer was £6,500 might have accepted £3,000 as simply what runway compensation costs. It is the comparison that converts an arbitrary figure into a visible injustice, and the manuscript is precise about why that comparison is so hard to press into anything more than resentment:

> *What they cannot prove is that the enforcement was chosen rather than triggered.*

Reverse the direction, from punishment to payment, and the logic holds. Stanwell residents cannot prove the £3,000 figure was chosen rather than simply generated by some internal Heathrow methodology nobody outside the process has seen. They can observe the outcome. They cannot audit the reasoning. And without the reasoning, there is no lever to pull, no clause to cite, no formula to challenge. There is only the number, and the neighbouring number that makes it look small.

The manuscript's account of what this kind of arrangement produces, over time, applies as much to compensation as it does to law:

> *These examples are not evidence of injustice; they are illustrations of how variance becomes structure. When success or failure is later explained entirely in moral terms, randomness has already been absorbed into the story.*

Give it a few years and £3,000 will simply be what Heathrow residents got, the way £6,500 will be what Gatwick residents got, and the difference between them will read as an unremarkable historical fact rather than the product of two separate, unaccountable internal decisions taken around the same time for the same kind of harm. The variance hardens into the record. Nobody has to defend it, because by the time anyone is asking, it will no longer look like a decision at all.
