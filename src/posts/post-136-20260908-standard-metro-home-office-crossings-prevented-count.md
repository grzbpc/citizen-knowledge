### The story

Protesters gathered in Portsmouth after migrants were landed in the city. Portsmouth council leader Steve Pitt criticised the Home Office for choosing "a completely unsuitable" place to land them, saying it "put people at risk and caused needless disruption". Hampshire Police said the activists left at around 4am, that no arrests had been made at that time, and that an investigation had been launched into assaults on officers, criminal damage to vehicles and other offences. A Home Office spokesperson said: "This government is bearing down on dangerous small boat crossings and restoring control at our border. Joint efforts have seen over 48,000 crossing attempts prevented and 1,100 boats and engines seized since the election." The spokesperson added that crossings were down this year, with "the lowest number recorded for the month of July since 2020", and that a payment-by-results deal with the French would "put 40% more boots on the ground on French beaches".

### The reframe

Whether the Government's policy on small boats is right is not the question here. The question is what kind of evidence the statement offers when it is asked about a particular event.

The council leader's complaint is specific. It concerns where people were landed and what followed on the night. The reply is general. It reports totals since the election: attempts prevented, boats and engines seized, officers promised. Each of those is a count of the department's own activity. An attempt prevented is, by definition, something that did not happen, so the figure rests entirely on how the department chooses to count it, and nobody at the other end is in a position to check. Boats and engines seized, and boots on the ground, are inputs. The payment-by-results deal is the one item tied to an outcome, though the statement does not say what the outcome is.

The only figure that describes what actually arrives is the one saying crossings are lower this year, and it is illustrated with a single month, July, the lowest since 2020. The statement then says "we must go further", which concedes that the measures have not yet produced the result while presenting the measures themselves as the evidence of progress.

None of this means the figures are wrong. It means they are the kind of figures an institution can defend internally whatever happens on a beach in Hampshire. The manuscript's account of enforcement describes metrics that are chosen because they can be defended, and reports that are written to be read upward. A landing on a particular night is a different sort of fact. It is visible, local and unarguable, and the reply to it was a spreadsheet.
