### The story

Take Five to Stop Fraud says more than half of people stayed on scam calls because they feared being rude. Such calls stole £92.4 million last year. The campaign urges people to "put protection before politeness". Spokesperson Jess Cook said: "Hang up, shut the door or simply say no." Separately, Gas Safe Register found that a quarter of Gen Z and Millennials judge a tradesperson on appearance over skills, against 14 per cent of older generations. Denise Van Outen said that "a clean van, smart uniform and five-star review might make someone look perfect" but that none of them tells you whether the person is qualified.

### The reframe

Both surveys describe the same thing. People have learned to read manners and presentation as signs that someone can be trusted, and a scammer or a poor tradesperson can supply both for almost nothing. Nobody ever taught anyone that a smart uniform proves nothing, or that putting the phone down on a stranger is acceptable. Both lessons now have to be delivered as a campaign, because the rule people absorbed first, be polite and judge by appearances, was the one that suited everyone except the person on the call.
