### The story

The Eiffel Tower has reopened after staff went on strike when women workers were barred for a visit by a Hindu delegation. Bosses said female staff at the Paris attraction should "not be in contact" with 100 members of the celibate Bochasanwasi Shri Akshar Purushottam Swaminarayan Sanstha sect, who were in the city to celebrate a new stone temple. CGT union leader Diane Davoine called the move "unacceptable". The mayor, Emmanuel Grégoire, promised an investigation, saying: "Gender equality will never stop at the foot of our historic monuments, or anywhere else in this city." The Hindu group later apologised to staff. Tower managers have not commented.

### The reframe

Two statements sit side by side. The mayor's is a principle, stated without exception. The managers' was an instruction to a group of employees, and the managers have not commented on it since. The group being hosted has apologised; the management has said nothing, which leaves the principle on the record and the instruction unexplained. Hosting a delegation with its own customs is an ordinary thing for a venue to do, and nothing in the report shows that the managers acted in bad faith. The point is narrower. A workplace was told it was governed by one rule and found it was operating under another, and it took a strike for the difference to be discussed in public.
