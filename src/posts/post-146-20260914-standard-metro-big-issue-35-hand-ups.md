### The story

The Prime Minister has given the Big Issue an exclusive interview for its 35th anniversary. Andy Burnham, who has vowed to end rough sleeping, said: "I can't believe how old it makes me feel now the Big Issue is turning 35. So many lives changed, so many lives lifted." The magazine has earned more than £162 million for its sellers, who are experiencing homelessness or poverty. Its founder, Lord John Bird, said: "We weren't interested in hand-outs. The Big Issue has always focused on hand-ups."

### The reframe

The Big Issue has done what it set out to do for a great many people, and nothing here is a criticism of it. The interest lies in the sentence Lord Bird uses to describe it. The distinction between a hand-out and a hand-up is also the distinction the manuscript traces through the moral treatment of work: help is respectable when it arrives in the form of employment, and suspect when it arrives as support. The seller earns the money by standing in a street and selling. That is the point of the model, and it is why it has public sympathy that a plain payment to the same person would not.

It also means that the model is open only to the people who can do the job. A person who is too unwell to sell is not served by a hand-up. The manuscript's concern is with how readily a society makes the second kind of help a matter of character, and how the first kind comes to seem like the only legitimate one.

Then there is the time. Thirty-five years is a long period for an organisation to have existed to address a condition that the Prime Minister has just promised to end. Both things can be true. The Big Issue can be a real success and the condition it serves can remain unresolved. The figure of £162 million is a large sum. Spread over 35 years it comes to a little over £4.6 million a year across all the sellers there have been, which gives some sense of scale against a problem a Prime Minister is promising to end. The report offers a birthday and a promise. It does not say what has changed, or how long the promise will take.
