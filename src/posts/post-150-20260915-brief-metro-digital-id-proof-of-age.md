### The story

Pubs and retailers will be able to accept digital ID as proof of age to buy alcohol under new rules. The change starts today in England and Wales and aims to provide an alternative to physical ID, so that people can use their phones to prove their age at the pub, at festivals and at concerts. The ID is available through third-party apps such as Yoti. In July, Metro reported that the Government's national digital ID scheme had been scrapped.

### The reframe

The national scheme went in July and a version of it has arrived in September, for one purpose, delivered by private companies. Nothing in the report suggests anything improper. A pub needs to know a single fact, that the customer is over 18, and a phone can in principle prove that without revealing a name or an address. A physical licence shows both. What the report does not say is what the apps record, what they keep, or whether the same credential will later be asked to prove something else. The manuscript's point is that privacy used to survive on difficulty, and that each convenient system now has to be designed to keep it. This is a small, sensible change, and it is a useful one to remember when the next use is proposed.
