### The story

Sharon Williamson, 59, a grandmother of ten from Leicester, won £141,000 on William Hill's online casino game in March. She was paid the prize in dribs and drabs, and has now been ordered to repay all of it after William Hill said her win was caused by a glitch. "I won fair and square," she said. "Now they're acting like I'm a criminal. It's so cruel to have it taken away." William Hill did not comment.

### The reframe

Betting operators are entitled to void a result that came from a genuine fault, and many sets of terms say so. The report does not say what the glitch was, how it was identified, or which terms William Hill relied on. All of that matters, and none of it can be judged from what has been printed. What can be said is how the arrangement is built.

The manuscript describes an online casino game as a designed product. The operator sets the return to the player, chooses how volatile the game is, and runs the system on which the outcomes are generated. A win is a legitimate result when the system says it is. Here the system said so in March, and the operator said otherwise months later. The outcome that was paid in instalments was later reclassified as a fault, by the party that owns the machine and holds the records. The player, who has only the screen she saw and the money she was sent, has no equivalent access.

The manuscript also describes what usually happens when the architecture produces harm. The responsibility goes to the individual and the design is protected. A player who loses too much is told to set limits. In this case it runs the other way round: if the operator's account is right, the design produced an error, and the cost is placed on the player. Sharon Williamson carried the risk of the game's outcomes, and has now been handed the risk of its faults as well, only after the first had gone in her favour.

There is a plain test that would settle most of it. If the operator can show that an error occurred, and show it to the player in terms she can check, the repayment has a basis. If it cannot, a payment that was made would need a better explanation for being taken back than a single word. The report ends with William Hill declining to comment, which means the account of the glitch, so far, is the company's alone.
