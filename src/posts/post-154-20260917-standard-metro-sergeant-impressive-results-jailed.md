### The story

A police officer who asked a workmate to send him a sex video of a female suspect and urged colleagues to "smash" a teenager's head in has been jailed for four years. Former sergeant Paul Street, 41, was said to have created a "toxic" culture at Cambourne Police Station, which tackled county lines drug supply and organised crime. The Cambridgeshire Constabulary officer was known for his "robust" style and "impressive results". Anti-corruption officers began a probe in 2021 after a new officer reported him, and two WhatsApp groups were found. The Old Bailey heard that in April 2020 he called on his team to "bully" a 17-year-old suspect. In another exchange he asked PC Josh Williams, 38, about an intimate private video of a female suspect, saying he wanted to show it to "the lads from footy". Williams was jailed for 16 months.

### The reframe

The two phrases the report uses to describe the man's reputation, "robust" and "impressive results", are the ones an institution can record. A station that tackles county lines drug supply and organised crime produces arrests, seizures and charges, and a sergeant whose team produces them is, on the measures available, doing well. The conduct in the WhatsApp groups does not appear on any of those measures. It could not, because the measures count outcomes and say nothing about how they were obtained.

The manuscript calls this success measured upward. Results are defensible internally and readable by those above the station, and they reward the supervisor who delivers them. The sergeant's style was called robust, which is a word that turns a method into a virtue provided the numbers are good.

Notice also how the matter came to light. A new officer reported him. That is someone with the least time in the station, and the least time spent learning what was ordinarily left unsaid. The report does not tell us whether anyone else had noticed earlier, or whether anyone had tried to raise it and not been heard. What it does record is that the messages were from April 2020, the probe began in 2021, and the sentence has been passed in 2026. The place in the chain that finally produced a report was the one nearest to the entrance.

This entry makes no claim about policing in general. The sergeant and the constable have been convicted and sentenced. What it describes is the mechanism by which a unit's results can stand in for an assessment of its conduct, and the detail that, here, the correction came from the person least invested in the numbers.
