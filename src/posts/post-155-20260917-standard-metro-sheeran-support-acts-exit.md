### The story

Ed Sheeran's US tour has been plunged into crisis after all his remaining support acts withdrew in solidarity with rapper Macklemore, who was dropped over pro-Palestinian remarks. Finneas, Irish singer-songwriter Aaron Rowe, Danish act Lukas Graham and Irish band Beoga have all pulled out. Macklemore was removed from the US leg of the tour after making a speech in support of Palestine in the conflict with Israel and showing images from Gaza during two shows in New Jersey. The performance drew criticism from several Jewish groups and from the singer Pink, who is Jewish. She said of the speech: "That scared me, and it scared people I love." In his own statement, Sheeran said stadium bosses had threatened to cancel shows if Macklemore stayed on the line-up, that it "was the promoter's decision, not mine", and that "Macklemore's contract for the tour was with the promoter, not me".

Finneas posted: "Artists must not be silenced when they speak up for the oppressed. I have decided to withdraw from my upcoming tour dates with Ed Sheeran." Rowe said he "cannot stand by and allow billionaires to use their position of power to silence the rightful voices of those who speak up". Beoga wrote that "to play in venues that silence anyone advocating for a Free Palestine is simply not an option for us." The tour has ten dates remaining, with a show in Philadelphia booked for Saturday. Sheeran had not yet addressed the latest departures.

### The reframe

This entry takes no side in the conflict, or in the argument about Macklemore's speech. It is about the shapes that a response to a decision can take, and this story has all of them.

The political economist Albert Hirschman described three: exit, voice and loyalty. The manuscript puts its emphasis on the commonest response of all, which is also the least visible: staying in place and saying nothing, which it treats as information and not as consent. In this story the acts chose exit, and each attached a statement, so it was exit with voice. Pink used voice from the other side of the argument about the speech. Sheeran's position, set out the day before, was that "just because I choose not to speak publicly, it doesn't mean I don't care". That is a claim about silence, and silence is the thing the manuscript warns against reading too quickly. It is not always agreement, and it is not always disapproval either. It is a response whose meaning is supplied by whoever is reading it.

Exit is the costly one. A support slot on a stadium tour is a large audience every night, and four acts gave it up within a day. The manuscript's argument is that when people withdraw, the withdrawal is information about the institution they are leaving, and that institutions tend to read it as an inconvenience. The decision at the centre of this story was attributed on the previous day to venues, then to a promoter, and not to the artist. By leaving, the acts declined to accept that chain as the end of the question.

Ten dates remain and the report records no statement from the promoter. The manuscript's last point is that a system receiving signals still has the chance to correct. The signals here are in the open and have been stated plainly. What happens next is the test of whether anyone in the chain treats them as information.
