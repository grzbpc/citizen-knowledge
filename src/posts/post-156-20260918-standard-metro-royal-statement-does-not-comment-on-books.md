### The story

Earl Spencer's new book, Swan Song: Diana, My Sister, claims that King Charles sounded "giddily elated" when he phoned to say Diana had died in 1997, and that he said "We'll forget her soon enough". The King gave no hint of his feelings over the book during an AI summit. The night before, his staff issued a statement: "While we do not comment on books as a matter of principle, His Majesty is mindful that the pain of fraternal grief can cloud reason, affect judgment and colour memory in ways others do not recognise, even many years after such a loss." The Prince and Princess of Wales spent the day on a litter pick at a beach and a trip to Scotland. Earl Spencer's book goes on sale on Tuesday.

### The reframe

This entry does not assess the claims in the book, which concern a private telephone call from 1997 that no reader of Metro can verify. It looks only at the statement.

The first clause says the household does not comment on books. The remainder comments on the book. It says nothing about whether the call took place, or what was said. It speaks instead to the state of mind of the person who has written about it: that grief can cloud reason, affect judgment and colour memory. That is a comment about the reliability of the author, delivered in a sentence that begins by saying nothing will be said. It is also considerate in tone and plainly addressed to a bereaved man, which is what makes it work as a reply. It gives the reader a reason to treat the book with caution without anyone having to deny a thing.

The manuscript's account of this kind of language is that it expands to fill the space where an answer would otherwise be required. A denial would need to be specific, and a specific denial can be tested. A statement about grief cannot. The principle of not commenting on books, stated openly, is a courteous position, and the statement then departs from it in the same breath. Both of those things can be true of a household under pressure. The point for the reader is that the sentence that carries the weight is the one that is not about the facts.

The statement leaves the question of the call exactly where it was. The book goes on sale on Tuesday.
