### The story

Meta is launching a camera-free version of its smart glasses after a backlash that saw the Ray-Ban collaboration branded "pervert glasses". The glasses, first unveiled in 2023, have been dogged by reports that they were used to film strangers secretly, raising fears of harassment against women and girls. The new model, Ray-Ban Meta Audio, keeps the audio abilities of the original but not the camera. Meta denied the product was rushed out after the backlash, saying it had been in the works for two years. Seven million of the original glasses were sold last year, starting at £220. Meta's safety guidelines say the device should not be used for "harassment, infringing on privacy rights, or capturing sensitive information". One woman told the BBC she was "humiliated" by a covertly filmed video of herself shared online and viewed up to 40,000 times. She said the man offered her a "paid service" when she asked for it to be removed. He told the BBC in an email that he "does not seek to cause distress or harm". The Met Police opened an investigation but was "unable to progress due to limited information". Wetherspoons, theatres, Comic-Con, schools and UK courts have banned the glasses.

### The reframe

Two responses sit side by side in the report. One is a rule: the guidelines say the glasses should not be used for harassment, and venues have banned them. The other is a product: a version with no camera. The first depends on someone complying and someone noticing. The second removes the thing that could be misused. The report sets the guidelines beside the woman's account and the police statement, and leaves the reader to compare them.
