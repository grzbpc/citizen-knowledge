### The story

Ten NHS staff have been "removed from duty" after East Suffolk and North Essex NHS Foundation Trust found that employees had tried to access the private patient records of a child whose death had been widely reported. The family has received an apology. The trust's deputy chief medical officer, Dr Martin Mansfield, called the acts "unacceptable" and said the trust had apologised unreservedly. The report describes the case as the latest involving NHS staff accessing the data of patients in high-profile cases. Staff who snoop on patient records now face immediate suspension, with NHS England chief executive Sir Jim Mackey ordering protection measures to be introduced at every trust.

### The reframe

The report says the staff tried to access the records. It does not say how the attempts were found. The plain reading is that the record system keeps a trace of who looks at what, and that someone examined it. If that is right, nobody had to be watching the staff in the old sense. The attempts became visible because the system that holds the information also holds a log of everyone who touched it.

The manuscript calls this visibility without observation. There need be no investigator and no one monitoring behaviour. A record of activity builds up as a by-product of ordinary use, and it can be read afterwards. Here it was used to find colleagues, not patients, which is a reminder that the same architecture records everyone who passes through it.

The second half of the story is the response. Staff who snoop will now be suspended at once, and every trust must put protection measures in place. Both are sensible, and nobody in the report argues otherwise. They also arrive in the usual order. The report calls this case the latest of its kind, which means the earlier ones did not produce measures that held at every trust. It also suggests that such measures were not already in place everywhere, though the report does not say so in terms.

One feature is worth keeping in view. The cases the report mentions are those involving high-profile patients, because those are the ones where someone has a reason to look and the public has a reason to hear about it. The records of every other patient sit in the same system. The new measures are described as applying to every trust, and the report gives no sign that they stop at the famous.
