### The story

Metro asked how long a coffee buys you in a cafe, and the answer is that the welcome grows lukewarm at much the same speed as the drink. Sean Kyne, owner of Alde Cafe in Aldeburgh, said people who hog tables are "annoying", and that staff ask if they would like more drinks and then give them the bill if they say no. Marianne Brammer, who runs HJEM in Kensington, gets round the problem by not offering WiFi, and said the cafes have an "unspoken rule" that "if it's super busy then regular guests know not to occupy tables for hours". Corgi Cafe in Shoreditch welcomes laptops. In the US, Chris Retallick of Boxwood Coffee Roasters said each receipt carries a WiFi password valid for four hours on weekdays, and knew of shops that limit it to 45 minutes.

### The reframe

The menu does not mention a time limit. The regulars know one exists and a first-time customer does not. The owners have tried different ways of making the rule real: a question about another drink, a missing password, a code that expires. Each is a way of enforcing a rule that has not been written down, and the person on the receiving end finds out when it is applied.
