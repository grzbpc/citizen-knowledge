### The story

Refugees from Palestine and women and girls from Afghanistan are to benefit from new safe passages to the UK announced by the home secretary, Shabana Mahmood. She told the Labour conference that offering "safe and legal" routes will deter small boat crossings and "unlock the inherent generosity of this country". The UK Resettlement Scheme will reopen immediately after being closed in 2024, with around a quarter of those admitted expected to be Palestinians and Afghan females. Three new sponsorship routes will also open, with communities, universities and companies able to apply to host refugees. Small boat crossings are at their lowest level in seven years. The Home Office is still to reveal the number at which arrivals under the new schemes will be capped, but said it will be in the "low thousands". Steve Valdez-Symonds of Amnesty UK said the moves would "offer a vital lifeline" but that "hundreds of places fall far short of what's needed". Shadow home secretary Chris Philp accused Ms Mahmood of "opening up our borders" to "appease Angela Rayner and the Labour left". The first arrivals sponsored by communities and universities are expected next autumn, and applications for employer-sponsored refugees will open in spring.

### The reframe

The speech attracted criticism from both sides at once. Amnesty says the places are too few and the shadow home secretary says the borders are being opened. Both are arguing about a number, and the number is the one thing not yet announced.

The manuscript has a short argument about how generosity is talked about. Public discussion focuses on intent and the size of the gesture, and pays little attention to proportion, meaning what is given relative to what could be. It does not say that gestures are worthless. It says that they can stand in for the measure.

In this case the language is generous, and the report quotes her using the words "inherent generosity" twice. The measure is given as "low thousands", a range and not a figure, with the cap to follow. Neither the ministers nor the critics have yet been in a position to argue about a number. The first arrivals are over a year away.

None of this is a criticism of the policy. A safe and legal route is a different thing from a dangerous crossing, and the report records that crossings are at their lowest level in seven years. The question for a reader is the one the speech did not answer: what the "low thousands" is measured against, whether it is the number of people in need, the number who crossed last year, or something else. A figure with no comparison attached is an announcement of intent. A figure with one is a policy.
