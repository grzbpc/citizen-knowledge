### The story

Angela Rayner, returning to conference as housing secretary, said she had "unfinished business" as she pledged to go further to protect leaseholders by placing a cap on administration fees. Homeowners can currently be charged hundreds of pounds for owning a pet or replacing a front door, she told delegates. She spoke of "confronting the scandal faced by millions of leaseholders who have found their dream of home ownership turned into a nightmare". She said: "We will legislate for an independent regulator to rein the cowboys in. They'll be required to follow the rules, and if they don't, their licences will be taken away."

### The reframe

Whether each charge is lawful is a question for the courts, and the report does not say. What it does describe is a person who owns a home, is asked for hundreds of pounds to replace a front door, and has little room to bargain. The manuscript's observation about unequal positions is a general one: the rules can be the same for both sides while one side can see the whole of the arrangement and the other meets it a charge at a time. The announced regulator and cap would change the position, but the size of the cap and the powers of the regulator are not yet given, so the remedy is still an intention.
