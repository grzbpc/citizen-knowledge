### The story

Prime minister Andy Burnham refused to rule out pausing a fuel duty rise planned for the new year, saying "every possible measure" is being considered to ease motoring costs. Diesel prices at the pump hit a record 199.53p per litre on Tuesday, and fuel duty on diesel is due to rise by 3p per litre on 1 January. Asked whether he would halt the rise, he said he was "considering everything that might help people", adding: "The question I've got is: How many can I afford?" Experts have warned that household energy bills could soar by £300 in January, and the prime minister said it was "not an overstatement" to describe the situation as an "energy crisis".

### The reframe

The answer is an honest one. A government with a fixed budget cannot promise every relief at once, and saying so is more direct than many replies of this kind. The manuscript makes a similar allowance when it describes how official language behaves under pressure.

The thing to notice is the shape of the answer. It does not say yes and it does not say no. It tells the listener that a decision is being weighed, which is true, and which also leaves the listener holding the same worry as before. No decision has been announced, and until one is the sentence is the nearest thing to a policy that anyone has.
