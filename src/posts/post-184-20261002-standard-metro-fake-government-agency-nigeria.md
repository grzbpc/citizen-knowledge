### The story

A Nigerian man has appeared in court accused of creating a fake government agency, installing himself as its boss and using it to collect over $1 million (£750,000) in funding. Adeniyi Adeyemi allegedly posed for almost three years as director-general of the Presidential Foreign Investment Promotion Council (PFIPC), which the presidency says does not exist. His social media posts show him using a government building to host a delegation from Thailand, including the country's ambassador, for talks on "strengthening bilateral trade relations". Prosecutors say he secured offices inside the federal secretariat and took $1 million in funding this year alone, and that government institutions processed his requests for space and staff "in the mistaken belief the PFIPC was genuine". The court heard that he fooled officials with a forged letter that appeared to have been issued by president Bola Tinubu and signed by his chief of staff. Mr Adeyemi pleaded not guilty to all eight charges, which include impersonation and forgery, and has said in interviews that the PFIPC was genuine.

### The reframe

These are allegations, and the case has not been heard. The reframe is about the mechanism the prosecutors describe, and it does not depend on the verdict.

The account is of a system in which each step was completed correctly. A letter arrived on the right paper, a request for space was processed, a request for staff was processed, a delegation was received. Each official can say that the previous stage had been done. The manuscript describes the structure in its chapter on professional work: a series of stages that are each competent and each defensible in isolation, with no stage responsible for asking the underlying question. In the manuscript the underlying question is whether the activity creates value. Here it is whether the body exists at all.

The point is not that the officials were foolish. It is that a process which confirms its own paperwork can be passed by anything that looks like paperwork. If the prosecution is right, it took almost three years for the question to be put to the one office that could answer it, the presidency, and the answer was that no such council exists. The case has attracted attention in Nigeria because of its ambition, and that is understandable. The more useful lesson is quieter. A system that checks the form of a request, and not its source, is working as designed until someone asks it to do something else.
