### The story

A claim against Google seeking more than £1 billion in damages for millions of consumers over "hidden charges" is going to trial in the UK. The class action, in the Competition Appeal Tribunal, is being brought on behalf of 20 million consumers and businesses who bought from the Google Play Store between 2015 and 2026. Liz Coll, of Connected Consumers, alleges that Google broke competition law by charging developers a commission of up to 30% and passing the fee to customers as a "hidden charge". "This trial is about getting that money back for people," she said.

### The reframe

The claim is an allegation, the tribunal has not ruled on it, and the report does not carry Google's answer. What can be said is limited to the description of the mechanism, and the mechanism is the useful part.

A person who buys an app sees a price. The claim is that the price included a commission paid by the developer to the platform and passed on, and that the buyer had no way of seeing it. If so, the buyer took part in a market in the ordinary way, paid the listed amount, and was never in a position to know what the amount contained. The manuscript's chapter on markets describes participation of that kind: required in practice, and rarely explained. It points out that people are told not to worry if they cannot explain how the system works.

That is why the length of the period matters. The claim covers eleven years, from 2015 to 2026, and the group is 20 million people. Any one buyer's share of the damages is likely to be small, which may be why no single buyer would have had reason to go to a tribunal. The class action is the only form in which a charge of this kind can be examined at all, which is itself a comment on how visible such charges are. The trial will decide whether the charge existed and whether it was unlawful. It will not change the fact that, for most of the period, the people paying it could not have told.
