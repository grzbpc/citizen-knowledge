### The story

Freeing inmates early to tackle prison overcrowding has seen the number of available jail spaces jump by 44% in a week, data shows. The Ministry of Justice figures show the number rose from 1,556 to 2,233. Over the same period the adult prison population fell from 87,499 to 86,779. Under the scheme, 720 people walked free on Thursday after serving a third of their sentence, and about 4,500 will be released over 12 months. Those excluded include people convicted of murder and manslaughter, causing or allowing the death of a child, child sex offences, rape and death by dangerous driving.

### The reframe

The figure is accurate and it answers the question the scheme was designed to answer, which is how many cells are free. A jump of 44% in a week is a large change in that number, and no one is claiming otherwise. The manuscript is concerned with what such a number leaves out. A count of spare places says nothing about whether the people who left will return, or about what happened to the people their offences touched. Those are different measures, and the scheme will be judged on them over a longer period. The first week has delivered the number that could be delivered in a week.
