### The story

Insurers uncovered fraud worth £1.34 billion last year, boosted by an 85% surge in fake travel claims, according to the Association of British Insurers. A total of 4,500 bogus holiday claims were exposed, with fraudsters trying to obtain £8.6 million. Across all sectors the ABI exposed 93,900 bogus claims, with an average value of £14,300, the second highest on record. Motor insurance fraud made up 55% of detected cases. Det Ch Insp Simon Klust, of the City of London Police, said: "Fraudsters are targeting much bigger payouts. More than 250 fraudulent claims are detected each day, and this is likely to be the tip of the iceberg." Mark Allen, head of fraud at the ABI, said: "Fraud pushes up costs for everyone." The figures emerged after Daniel Thomas was jailed for four and a half years for attempting to make bogus claims for more than £300,000.

### The reframe

The report does not say how the claims were found, so this entry cannot say either. It shows a detection system working at volume, 250 cases a day, and a police officer's estimate that this is only part of the total. That estimate cannot be checked, because nobody knows the number that were not found.

The manuscript's chapter on administrative classification describes how such systems tend to work. They do not need to be sure about any one person; they need a pattern that is right often enough to be useful. The ABI's point about costs is fair. What the person on the other side of the pattern may not see is the classification itself, and the report gives no figure for how often a genuine claim is mistaken for a false one.
