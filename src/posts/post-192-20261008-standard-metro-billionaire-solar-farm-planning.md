### The story

A hedge-fund billionaire has been given the green light for a solar farm at his 100-bed mansion after winning a planning row with neighbours. Chris Rokos, who has been renovating Tottenham House in Marlborough, Wiltshire, wants to install 1,176 panels as "a sustainable form of power and water" for the house, which was built in 1575. Neighbours approve of his efforts to restore the mansion but object to the array. One said it "will be situated in an area of natural beauty" and a Grade 2* registered park and garden next to a Capability Brown landscape, and that "the glare from a three acre block of glass will massively change the outlook" from a protected stable block. Wiltshire council has backed the project at the former home of the Earl of Cardigan.

### The reframe

A planning decision is a judgement. A council weighs the objections against the benefits and decides, and the report gives no sign that Wiltshire did anything other than that. Solar power at a large house is a reasonable thing to propose, and the neighbours' objection is a reasonable one too. The facts do not point to a wrong decision.

The manuscript's interest is in the skill that sits around such a decision. The planning system is a set of rules, written for anyone, and it is used better by some applicants than others. The ability to present a scheme in the language the process rewards, to answer objections at the right stage, to know which points matter, is a form of literacy that is never formally taught. The manuscript says that those who grasp it early move through institutions with relative ease. It does not say that they are given anything they are not entitled to.

In this case the applicant wanted a particular result and has obtained it. The report cannot tell us whether the outcome would have been the same for an applicant with fewer resources. What it does show is a decision that went one way where reasonable people disagreed, and the useful question is who in the area has the means to contest such a decision.
