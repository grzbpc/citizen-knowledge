### The story

About 700 people have been arrested in a major operation targeting mobile phone thieves in London. Hundreds of suspected stolen phones were seized, along with drugs, knives, guns, cash and more than 110 illegal e-bikes. The Met said it had seen adverts on Snapchat offering children as much as £380 to steal a single iPhone, with a bonus of £100 for stealing ten. The international trade in stolen phones is worth millions, with a device taken from London worth more in countries such as China, where devices are fitted with restrictions imposed by the government. London has been described as "Europe's phone snatching capital", with about 80,000 devices stolen annually. Met commissioner Sir Mark Rowley has pushed for phone firms to add functions that would make stolen handsets "unusable bricks". Commander Andy Featherstone said: "Phone theft offences are at a four-year low because of the Met's relentless campaign to crack down on these criminals."

### The reframe

The arrests are a real result. People who steal phones were caught, goods were taken off the street, and the force has been consistent in making this a priority. None of that is in doubt.

The manuscript is interested in a quieter question, which is how an enforcement system describes its own success. Once a system reaches a certain scale, the number it can show most easily is its activity: 700 arrests, 110 e-bikes, hundreds of phones. That is a count of what the police did. It is not a count of what happened to the problem, and the report sets the two side by side without joining them. About 80,000 phones are still stolen in a year, the force says the rate is at a four-year low, and it attributes the fall to its own campaign, which is a claim that the report does not test.

The commissioner's remark about phone firms points the same way. If handsets could be made useless once stolen, the market that pays children £380 a phone would be harder to sustain, and the police would no longer be the only line of defence. That is a change that would be made by someone other than the police. It is the sort of fix an arrest total does not show, and it is worth watching for as a different kind of evidence that the problem is getting smaller.
