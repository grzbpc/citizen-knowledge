### The story

More protests have erupted in Spain after the death in hospital of 87-year-old María del Carmen Abascal, whose eviction from her long-time Madrid home last month because she could not afford a steep rent rise sparked nationwide unrest. "Maricarmen" became a symbol for renters struggling with housing affordability. Yesterday, activists near parliament said: "Maricarmen lives, the fight goes on!"

### The reframe

The report does not give the cause of her death, and this entry draws no link between it and the eviction. The two facts it does give are that she was removed from her home because of a rent rise she could not afford, and that her case became a national symbol. The manuscript's observation is about where the rigid application of rules tends to fall. The report does not say whether the eviction followed the law in every respect. The point applies either way: when a rent rise is enforced, the person with the least margin to absorb it is the one who has to leave. The protests suggest that many people recognised the pattern. Whether any law should change is for Spain to decide, and the report does not say what the protesters are asking for beyond the slogan.
