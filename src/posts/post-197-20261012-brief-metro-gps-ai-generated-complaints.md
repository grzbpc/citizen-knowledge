### The story

Patients are piling pressure on overworked GPs by sending them 20-page complaints generated by artificial intelligence, an industry body reports. Only 7% of family doctors have not encountered the problem, according to a poll by the Medical Protection Society, and 45% said the AI-generated letters had increased their workloads and harmed staff well-being and morale. Prof Victoria Tzortziou Brown, president of the Royal College of GPs, said technology risked producing complaints that were "complex or contain inaccurate information". The Public Service Ombudsman has issued guidance telling patients that "simple, clear language is always more helpful than complicated, formal writing".

### The reframe

A person with a genuine grievance may find that software helps them to say what they could not say alone, and nothing in the report suggests otherwise. The difficulty is on the receiving end. A complaint that runs to 20 pages has to be read, answered and recorded, and the effort of producing it has fallen while the effort of dealing with it has not. The manuscript's concern is with signals that are easy to produce in volume and difficult to turn into a result. The ombudsman's advice points the other way: a short letter that says what happened is usually of more use than a long one that sounds formal.
