### The story

Precarious low-paid jobs have pushed millions into debt and made them use food banks, says Citizens Advice. A third of workers aged 18 to 24 are among the 5.6 million "at the mercy" of insecure work, including those in transport and hospitality, cleaners and younger construction workers. Disabled people are "over-represented", the charity added, after a survey of 4,000 people. More than half of insecure worker households used credit, borrowed from family or friends, sold things to make ends meet, used a food bank or applied for crisis funds. The charity is demanding help with bills for the lowest earners.

### The reframe

The finding is from a charity that campaigns on the subject, and the survey is of 4,000 people, so the figures are best read as its measure of the problem. The point it makes is a simple one. These are people in work, in the sense the public usually means, and a job has not been enough. The manuscript describes a culture in which employment stands in for character, and where having work is taken as a sign of responsibility. If that is the assumption, then a household that has a job and still turns to a food bank does not fit the account, and the usual response is to look for something that the household did wrong. The survey asks a different question, which is what the job pays and how secure it is.
