### The story

A poll has found that many people would hesitate to perform CPR on a woman if her heart stopped. Three in ten worry they would be accused of inappropriate touching, and a fifth would be concerned that someone might film them pumping a stranger's bare chest, St John Ambulance reports. Medics warn that hesitation costs lives. About 40,000 attempts to resuscitate heart attack patients are made each year, and the estimated survival rate for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest is less than one in ten. The charity's medical director, Dr Lynn Thomas, said: "Every second counts in a cardiac arrest and doing something is always better than doing nothing." She added that it is important not to shame anyone for hesitating, as "these fears are real and understandable".

### The reframe

The fear in the poll is about how an act will be read afterwards, and not about the act. A person who would otherwise help stops to consider whether a bystander, a camera or a later complaint will interpret it differently from how they mean it. The manuscript describes this habit as a wider one, in which people adjust their conduct for an audience that may or may not exist. The charity's answer is a plain one: the person on the ground needs help now, and the worry should not come first. Its medical director also does not ask for the fear to be dismissed, which is the more honest way to put it.
