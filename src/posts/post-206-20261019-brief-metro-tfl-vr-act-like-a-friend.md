### The story

In a corner of Euston Station, Transport for London staff are giving advice on how best to react if you witness or suffer a hate crime or sexual harassment. Using virtual reality headsets, commuters are put in the middle of uncomfortable scenarios and shown how to behave as an "active bystander". Selecting "Act Like a Friend" is the safest option and the one TfL wants to instil in all Londoners: wait for the perpetrators to move on, take note of what they look like, comfort the victim and then report the incident. Intervening is strongly discouraged. TfL's head of safeguarding, Emma Croxall, agreed some crimes are underreported and said hate crime incidents are up 36% year on year. London TravelWatch estimates that two in five crime victims do not report what happens to them on public transport.

### The reframe

The advice is practical and it is sensible. A person who steps between a victim and an aggressor can be hurt, and the scenario in the film ends before the blow lands. TfL is teaching people to be safe and to give the system what it needs, which is a report.

The manuscript has a chapter about how people learn what no institution formally teaches them. It describes some of that learning as a kind of preparation: a rehearsal for dealing with a system that behaves differently from its description. The VR film is an open, literal version of that. It tells commuters what the system can and cannot do for them at the moment it matters. The rule says that hate crime is an offence. The advice says to wait, note and report.
