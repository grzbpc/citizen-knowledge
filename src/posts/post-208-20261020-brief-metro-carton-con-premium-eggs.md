### The story

A man was left "spitting feathers" after discovering that his premium eggs had been swapped for cheaper ones, in what he calls a "very middle-class crime". Andrew Evans, 32, said his wife bought a six-pack of Burford Brown eggs, which cost £3.60, from a Sainsbury's in north London. When he opened the box the next morning he found six plain white eggs. He believes another shopper swapped the contents, a "carton con" that let them buy the premium eggs for the price of a regular pack (Sainsbury's own brand cost £2.10). "It's a sign that people are unhappy with the prices of items in the shop, but it's also a lack of respect for fellow community members," he said. Sainsbury's threatened in April to prosecute thieves after a spate of incidents, and offered a refund.

### The reframe

Mr Evans may be right that the eggs were swapped in the shop, although the report cannot show it. If so, the act is theft, and it is a good example of the kind of offence that most people would describe in other words. The manuscript notes that few people see themselves as criminals even when they have done something that was technically illegal, and that most have a ready explanation for why their own case was different. A carton swap is easy to excuse because the loss falls on a shop and costs a few pounds. It is also, for the same reason, an offence that is rarely seen, and the company's threat of prosecution in April suggests it has become frequent enough to be worth a warning.
