### The story

Former health secretary Matt Hancock's claim that a "protective ring" had been thrown around care homes in the Covid crisis was "unfounded", Baroness Hallett has found. Her inquiry's sixth report said the claim did "not accurately reflect the lack of action taken" by mid-April 2020. Protecting residents was "an afterthought... with devastating consequences", Metro reports the inquiry as saying, as thousands were moved in from hospitals to free up beds, with no testing or guidance to isolate them. Staff without proper PPE had to wear rock festival ponchos, painters' overalls or bin bags. More than 43,000 people in care died of Covid from March 2020 to July 2022.

The report recognises that ministers faced tough decisions. By late January 2020 it was clear that people with no symptoms could spread Covid, and all four UK nations knew about the "vulnerability" of care home residents. Jean Adamson, of Covid-19 Bereaved Families for Justice, said the report was proof of Mr Hancock's "cruel lie", adding: "They made a decision that became a death sentence for our family members." Metro's account carries no response from Mr Hancock.

### The reframe, with the manuscript

The inquiry's finding is about a sentence. It does not say that ministers did nothing, and it recognises that they faced hard choices. It says that one phrase, "protective ring", did not match what had been done by a particular date. The question for this entry is what kind of failure that is, and the manuscript has a way of putting it.

> *Citizens may disagree with those decisions, but there remains a recognisable relationship between what is said and what actually happens.*

The manuscript's section on language describes the normal condition of an institution: its words explain decisions, set out objectives and describe outcomes. People can disagree with the decisions and still understand the words. The inquiry's account is of a period in which the words were out in front of the actions. A phrase such as "protective ring" gives a picture of something already built. The report says that on the date in question the building had not started.

The manuscript says what follows when this happens.

> *Problems begin when that relationship weakens.*

It goes on to say that this does not require anyone to lie. Under pressure, with outcomes hard to deliver, it says, the gap emerges naturally from the way institutions are placed. That is a general observation and not a finding about Mr Hancock. The bereaved families draw a sharper conclusion, and the word "lie" is theirs, a description of how the claim felt to those who lost relatives. The inquiry's own word is "unfounded", which is a statement about the evidence for the phrase and not about the intention behind it.

The manuscript names the effect on the public.

> *It emerges naturally from institutional pressure.*

It adds that people stop listening for what is being proposed and start listening for what the statement reveals about the pressure the speaker is under. What the inquiry has done is test the phrase against the record, and what remains is the simple point that a promise about protection is only as good as the action it describes.
