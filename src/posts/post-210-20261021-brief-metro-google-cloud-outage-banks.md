### The story

Google apologised after its cloud network went down, causing major disruption to online services and affecting thousands of people. Revolut, which has millions of UK customers, reported disruption to card payments, transfers, cash withdrawals and signing in. Hundreds of Lloyds Bank and HSBC users reported issues to Downdetector, as did users of Royal Mail, DPD, Rightmove and others. HM Courts & Tribunals Service had problems with the cloud video platform used for video links to court hearings. A Revolut user told the BBC the issues were a "fiasco" and that he was "starting to lose trust". He said: "I sent some money to my Revolut in order to make an online payment. I'm still waiting. I cannot do any food shopping until this fiasco is sorted out." Google blamed a "power event" on Monday evening in a region believed to be London, and said normal service was restored "for most" by Tuesday afternoon.

### The reframe

The fault was in a data centre, and the people affected were customers of banks and firms that depend on the service. The manuscript has a short line about how people pay, and it applies without any claim of surveillance here. When nearly everything is paid for electronically, a failure in a system that most of the public has never seen becomes a failure at the supermarket till. The user's complaint is plain: he had the money, and could not use it. The report does not say whether anyone lost anything permanently, and by afternoon most services were back.
