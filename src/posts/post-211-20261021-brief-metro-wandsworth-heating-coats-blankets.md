### The story

Coats, blankets and soup had to be ordered for inmates when the heating failed at a Victorian-era prison, a report has revealed. Temperatures at Wandsworth, south-west London, fell to 12C in November last year. The Independent Monitoring Board warned that the issue will be repeated until the heating system is replaced, and said conditions in the jail are "unsafe, unsanitary and squalid".

### The reframe

The response to the cold was a fast and sensible one. People were given coats, blankets and hot food. The monitoring board's point is about the next winter. The measures dealt with the cold on the day, and the cause, a heating system the board says needs replacing, was left as it was. The manuscript has a short line about why temporary measures tend to multiply. They can be ordered and withdrawn without any decision about the arrangement they cover. The report does not say whether replacement has been funded or scheduled, and that is the figure to look for before the weather turns.
