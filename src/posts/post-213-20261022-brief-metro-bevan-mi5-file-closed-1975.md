### The story

Aneurin "Nye" Bevan, one of the heroes of the Labour movement and the founder of the NHS, was subject to monitoring by MI5, newly released papers show. He first came to the attention of the security service in the 1930s amid concerns over communist links. The monitoring continued after he became a minister in the post-war Labour government, when he drove the creation of the NHS despite opposition from doctors. The head of MI5 even met prime minister Clement Attlee over Mr Bevan's links to a Russian spy. The files also show some agents questioning the surveillance, but the file was not closed until 1975, 15 years after Mr Bevan's death.

### The reframe

The papers are old, and the report gives only a short summary, so this entry cannot judge what the security service had in front of it at the time. The detail that stands out is a plain one. Some of the people doing the monitoring questioned it, and the file stayed open anyway for another decade and a half after the subject had died. The manuscript describes how systems built on records tend to see. They collect the pattern, a set of associations, and the context the person would have given is not in the file. What the Bevan papers show is that such a record can outlast the person and the reasons for keeping it.
