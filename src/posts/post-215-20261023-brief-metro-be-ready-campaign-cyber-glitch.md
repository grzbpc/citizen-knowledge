### The story

A global flaw in the internet, found by AI but accidentally leaked online, has led to millions of attempted hacks, Metro reports. The PHP flaw puts government services, businesses and private users at risk, tech experts say. In the UK, 32 government services were down and four partly shut, and small firms were affected, including garages that could not file MOTs. Cloudflare told of "millions" of hacking attempts on its network. The threat came as Britain launched the Be Ready campaign, telling families to keep three days of water, tinned food, cash, a radio and a torch in case of war, extreme weather or a cyber attack.

### The reframe

The advice is practical, and the report does not suggest otherwise. What is worth noticing is the word "cash" on a list of things to keep at home. The manuscript describes cash as the fallback people imagine when digital systems stop. On the day services went down, the official answer was to hold some. It treats the digital system as the normal condition and the paper note as the thing for when it fails.
