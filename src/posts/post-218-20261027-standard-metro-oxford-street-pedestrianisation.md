### The story

London mayor Sadiq Khan posed on Oxford Street as his pedestrianisation scheme began. Buses, cars, motorbikes and cycles are now barred from Orchard Street to Great Portland Street. Metro reports that some cyclists ignored the rules, and that critics fear the scheme will cause traffic chaos. Mr Khan called it a "bold vision" which will put the area on a par with New York's Times Square and Paris's Champs-Élysées, and said it will help transform the shop experience.

### The reframe

The scheme is a transport and planning decision, and this entry takes no side on whether it is a good one. Its interest is the first-day detail that some cyclists ignored the rules. One day tells us very little, and the report does not say how many, whether they were warned or what the penalty is.

The manuscript's point is about what happens whenever a new rule meets ordinary behaviour. Most people, it says, do not reject a rule. They learn how it operates in practice and adjust to that, and the gap between the written standard and the working one is acquired through experience and shared quietly. A restriction on a famous street will be tested in the same way. Which of the two versions of the rule people come to follow will depend on whether it is enforced, and the first day does not answer that.
