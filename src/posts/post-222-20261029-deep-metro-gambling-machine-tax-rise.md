### The story

Metro's Budget coverage reports that taxes on gambling will rise from 20% to 25%, and on gambling machines in particular from 20% to 25%, to pay for a high street support package. The report says the rise will help more retail, hospitality and leisure firms avoid business rates. Chancellor John Healey is also reported to be exempting vape shops and adult gaming centres from the business rates relief.

The same page of the Budget coverage reports a tax-free trading allowance rising from £1,000 to £3,000, a £10 million tax relief for small British brewers and cider makers, and a freeze on fuel duty. Metro's account of the gambling measure describes what the money is for. It does not report any measure aimed at reducing gambling harm, and it does not say how much the rise is expected to raise.

### The reframe, with the manuscript

The Budget is a list of who pays and who is relieved, and the gambling line is one entry on it. The entry asks what the line shows about how a harm is treated, and the manuscript has a section on exactly that.

> *The distinction between these categories is not determined by the severity of harm. It is determined by whether the harm has been administratively absorbed into the fiscal structure.*

On the manuscript's account, a harmful activity is either prohibited and policed or licensed and taxed, and the choice follows what the activity does for the public finances. Metro's report fits that pattern at the level of what it says. Gambling appears in it as a source of money for another purpose, and the purpose is relief for shops, pubs and leisure businesses. The harm is not the subject of the sentence.

The manuscript is direct about what this means.

> *This creates a structural dependency. The state does not merely tolerate these harms. It relies on them financially.*

A higher rate does not by itself show dependency, and a tax can be set high on purpose to discourage a product. Metro's report gives no such reason. What it does show is that the revenue has been given a job in the same announcement. Once a tax is spending money for a policy elsewhere, a fall in gambling would leave a gap in that policy, and that is the dependency the manuscript describes.

The vape shops and adult gaming centres exempted from the relief are a small illustration of the other half. The business rates relief is for the high street businesses the Chancellor wants to protect, and the Budget draws the line around them by type. Whether the line is justified is a question for the people who drew it. The entry notes only that it is drawn by category.

The manuscript's description of what the system does with harm applies to this setting.

> *This is containment, not resolution.*

The sentence belongs to the manuscript's discussion of harm mitigation, where the harm is acknowledged, the revenue is kept and the system carries on. Metro's report carries no acknowledgement of harm to hold up against it. The reader is left with a rate going up and a relief being paid for, and the question of who bears the cost of the activity that produces the money is not raised on the page.
