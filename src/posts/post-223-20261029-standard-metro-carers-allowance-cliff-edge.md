### The story

Metro's Budget guide sets out how Carer's Allowance works. To claim it, a person generally needs to care for someone for at least 35 hours a week and earn no more than £204 a week after tax, National Insurance and allowable expenses. It pays £86.45 a week, but, Metro explains, it can drop to nothing if the carer earns even slightly more than allowed.

Chancellor John Healey said earnings above the limit will be subject to a taper that reduces the payment as earnings increase. The taper will not be implemented until 2030-31, following the development of an IT system that will automatically adjust pay. Metro's worked example is a carer called Louise, who looks after her mother. If she earns even a penny over the limit she can lose the whole payment. Under the new rules, if she earned £30 a week over the limit she would keep £56.45 of her allowance instead of losing all £86.45, which Metro calculates would leave her almost £3,000 better off over a year.

### The reframe

The earnings limit is a line, and the payment falls from £86.45 to nothing at the point where a carer crosses it. Metro's guide treats this as a feature of the current rules and describes the Budget's answer as a fix that arrives in 2030-31.

The manuscript's chapter on welfare describes how conditional support tends to be enforced: with a rigidity that is absent elsewhere, where minor infractions trigger sanctions and administrative failure is turned into personal irresponsibility. A cliff edge of this kind is a clear case of the first part. The amount by which a carer is over the limit does not matter; the payment goes entirely. The entry does not say the limit is unjustified, and a limit of some sort is a normal part of a means-tested benefit. The point is the shape of it.

Two things are worth setting beside each other. One is the size of the sum: £30 over the limit costs £86.45, which is nearly three times the extra earnings. The other is the reason given for the delay. The taper has been announced, and the date is set by the time needed to build an IT system. The manuscript's description of a welfare system whose rules carry on regardless of how well the process works applies here at the level of the calendar. For five more years, the rule stays as it is, and the carer with a small amount of extra work is the one who has to manage the line.

Metro's report is about the policy and one illustrative case. It gives no figure for how many carers sit near the limit, so the entry does not claim the cliff edge catches large numbers of people. It records that the Budget has named the problem, set out the remedy and put the start of it in 2030-31.
