### The story

Chancellor John Healey has scrapped plans to cut the annual Cash ISA allowance for under-65s from £20,000 to £12,000, Metro reports. The cut was announced last year by his predecessor Rachel Reeves and was due to take effect in April 2027. It was designed to encourage investment in the stock market, and had drawn criticism from finance experts who said people might simply leave money sitting in non-ISA accounts. Mr Healey said the allowance will stay at £20,000, and that he will work with the sector to create a new starter ISA to reward first-time investors. Metro's guide adds that the Chancellor will explore a new incentive for people investing in a stocks and shares ISA for the first time, and that there are no details yet and experts have called for clarification.

### The reframe

The reversal is clear: the cut will not go ahead. The replacement is not. Metro reports an incentive that is to be explored and a starter ISA that is to be created with the sector, and for neither is there a rate, a date or a limit. The manuscript describes how a government can say it has an answer to a problem before the answer exists. Here the first part has been settled and the second is still an intention. The entry records both as Metro does, and leaves the question of whether the new incentive will arrive to the details that have not yet been published.
