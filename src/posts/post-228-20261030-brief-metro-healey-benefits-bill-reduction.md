### The story

Andy Burnham and John Healey went to a Rolls-Royce plant in Derby hoping to talk about a £2.5 billion jet engine deal, and were pressed instead on the rising cost of the welfare bill, Metro reports. Asked by the BBC whether benefits would be cut before the next election, the chancellor said "There will be a reduction", and that it would be done in a Labour way. He called the welfare system unsustainable. The reviews that would set out changes, one led by Alan Milburn on young people and work and one by Stephen Timms on disability benefits, have yet to report. No.10 said the government has not set a timetable. The Office for Budget Responsibility has warned that sickness and disability benefits could cost £100 billion a year by the end of the decade. Treasury officials dismissed reports that Mr Healey wants cuts before the next election.

### The reframe

The OBR warning is a fair reason to discuss spending, and the entry does not say the bill should not fall. It notes what has been fixed and what has not. The aim is stated as a total, the bill coming down, and the measures that would deliver it sit in reviews that have not published. Metro's report gives no account of what the change would mean for the people who claim. The manuscript says that in a system of conditional support, the measure of success tends to be the figure on the bill.
