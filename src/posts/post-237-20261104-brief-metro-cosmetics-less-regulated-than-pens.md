### The story

Metro's page 5 reports that hair and beauty products are less regulated than ballpoint pens, and that gaps in controls are causing avoidable harm, according to MPs. The Science and Technology Committee described the existing regulations as confusing and said they had failed to keep up with the sector. People have reported chemical burns, infections and allergic reactions after using cosmetics or having procedures. Metro adds that the report follows proposals for Brazilian butt lifts to be classed as surgery.

### The reframe

The paragraph reports a committee finding and no response from government or industry, and it does not say how the comparison with ballpoint pens was made, so the entry cannot test it. It can note what the comparison asserts: that products applied to skin and hair sit in a lighter category than a pen. The manuscript's argument on classification is that categories lose their authority when they stop reflecting the principles used to justify them, and a proposal to class a cosmetic procedure as surgery is a reclassification of exactly that kind. Nothing reported suggests the gap was drawn to serve revenue, which is the manuscript's main example, so this is a milder case, in which a category has fallen behind the practice it covers. Whether the proposals proceed is what to watch.
