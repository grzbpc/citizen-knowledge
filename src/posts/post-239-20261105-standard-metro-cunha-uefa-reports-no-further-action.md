### The story

Metro reports on pages 34 and 35 that Matheus Cunha has avoided any further punishment after coming back onto the pitch at the end of Manchester United's Champions League win over Roma, despite having been sent off. Cunha scored twice and won a penalty in the 3-2 comeback, then was shown a second yellow card in injury time, the first for taking his shirt off after scoring the winner and the second for a foul on Leonardo Balerdi. Instead of leaving by the tunnel, he sat on an advertising board at the Stretford End to watch the last few minutes and walked back onto the pitch after the final whistle.

United staff spotted the potential issue and the manager, Michael Carrick, ordered him back to the dressing room. Metro says Cunha reacted angrily to being told by Carrick and his staff not to celebrate with his team-mates on the grass, because he risked breaking Uefa rules and incurring more than the automatic one-match ban. A Uefa spokesman confirmed that Cunha faces no additional action and will miss only the next Champions League game, against Sporting Lisbon. He said that Uefa's official reports did not mention the incident, so the automatic ban should not be extended, and that Uefa is not investigating further.

### The reframe

The entry does not assess whether any rule was broken. Metro does not say which rule was at issue, and a player's conduct after a dismissal is a matter on which governing bodies have their own provisions. The narrower point is what the outcome turned on, and the paper reports it plainly: it was what the official reports recorded, and not what the player did.

The manuscript's account of threshold logic describes behaviour reorganising around the question of whether an act is close enough to attract attention, and not around whether it is within the rule. The page shows that question being asked in real time. United's staff identified a risk that the player did not, and acted on it to prevent a penalty that would have followed had it been recorded. Uefa's answer, as reported, is that nothing was recorded and so nothing will be pursued. The manuscript observes that people in this position cannot prove whether enforcement was chosen or merely triggered, and here the body concerned says in terms that it was a matter of trigger.

Two things cut against the simple reading. A system that acts on official reports is a design, not an accident, and nothing in Metro's account suggests that Cunha was treated differently from any other player in the same position. The reported outcome also looks lenient only if the conduct was a breach, which the paper does not establish. The entry therefore records a partial fit, in which the pattern is clear and the evidence of selectivity is not. Whether a different club, or a different player, would have been treated alike is a question that this page cannot answer.
