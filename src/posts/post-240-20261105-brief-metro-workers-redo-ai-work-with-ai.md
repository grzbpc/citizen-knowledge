### The story

A Metro Bites item on page 13 reports that half of workers have redone colleagues' work they believe was done using AI, mostly by cutting waffle and correcting facts. The clean-ups take an average of six hours and 57 minutes a week, according to a poll by Arden University. The paper adds, as an irony, that half of them sorted out the shoddy work by using AI.

### The reframe

The item reports a poll of what workers believe, and gives no sample size or method, so the entry cannot test the figures. It can note the shape they describe: output produced quickly, then corrected by a colleague, often with the same kind of tool, at a cost the paper puts at nearly a working day a week. The manuscript's chapter on professional loops describes work whose main function is to validate or repair other work, with no external point at which anyone checks that anything has improved. The poll does not show that, since the corrections may well improve the final product, and the paper does not say whether they do. It does show a loop of the kind the chapter describes, in a literal and mild form, and the entry offers it as an illustration and nothing stronger.
