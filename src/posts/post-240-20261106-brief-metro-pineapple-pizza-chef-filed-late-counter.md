### The story

Metro reports on page 6 that Davide Tamburella, an Italian chef, was dismissed from the La Costa restaurant in Brisbane in May after telling his boss that putting pineapple on a pizza was an insult to his culture. It was the third time in four years that he had refused. Other reasons given for the dismissal included inappropriate language after he fractured his fingers hitting a metal handrail, according to the Fair Work Commission ruling. His claim of unfair dismissal was dismissed because he had filed it too late. His former boss said there was more to the story than a pizza topping, and Metro adds that he has since become head chef at another Italian restaurant.

### The reframe

This looked, on a first pass, as though it might belong here: a worker, a seemingly trivial reason for dismissal, and an institution that declined to hear the claim. But the outcome turned on a filing deadline, which is a stated and fixed limit, and nothing in the report suggests it was applied to him differently from anyone else. The manuscript's threshold logic concerns boundaries that are unstated and movable, because clear thresholds would constrain discretion and opaque ones expand it; a closed time limit is the opposite case. Metro does not say why the claim was late, or whether the merits would have been heard had it been in time, so the entry draws nothing from the dismissal itself. It is included, briefly, so that a story with a surface resemblance to the project's material is not counted as evidence.
