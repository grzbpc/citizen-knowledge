### The story

Metro reports on page 2 that Nigel Farage has suggested he believes Parliament's standards commissioner will find against him over a £5 million gift from the Thai-based crypto tycoon Christopher Harborne. The commissioner, Daniel Greenberg, interviewed Mr Farage in September. Under Commons rules, new MPs must register any gift worth more than £300 within a year. Asked on the BBC's Sunday With Laura Kuenssberg whether he expected the commissioner to find against him, Mr Farage said, "Do I think the establishment would find against me, the leader of an insurgent party? What a question to ask." He has claimed the money was to pay for his security.

### The reframe

This looked, on a first pass, as though it might belong here: a figure with considerable standing, an oversight body, and a claim that the outcome is already settled. But the page shows a rule with a stated threshold and a stated time limit, a named commissioner who has interviewed the person concerned, and an inquiry that has not reached a finding. The manuscript's concern is with how a rule is used, whatever it says, and on that question the report has nothing yet to offer: no outcome, no comparison with how others were treated, and no evidence for or against Mr Farage's expectation, which is stated as a belief and not as a fact. The entry takes no view on whether the gift was properly declared or whether the security explanation is sufficient, since neither is established on the page. It is included, briefly, so that a story with a surface resemblance to the project's material is not counted as evidence, and because the finding, when it comes, would be the first real test of how the rule is applied.
