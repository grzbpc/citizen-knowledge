### The story

Metro reports on pages 34 and 35 that Michael Carrick's future as Manchester United's manager is due to be decided today, when the chief executive, Omar Berrada, and the director of football, Jason Wilcox, meet the minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco. The paper says all the indications are that Mr Carrick will be sacked. United are in the relegation zone, have won one of their last ten Premier League games, their worst start since Sir Alex Ferguson replaced Ron Atkinson 40 years ago, and have not won a top-flight match since August. Mr Carrick was given a two-year contract in May after guiding the club to third place and Champions League qualification as interim coach. Metro adds that United sources say no final decision has been made, that a dismissal would mean searching for the club's eighth permanent manager or head coach since Ferguson retired, and that Mauricio Pochettino is reported to be the frontrunner, with a release clause of around £7 million.

### The reframe

This looked, on a first pass, as though it might belong with the entries on failure being attributed to individuals, because a manager given a long contract in May may be removed in November, and the club has had a run of managers. But the page shows an ordinary form of accountability. The stated ground is a league record set out in figures the paper itself relies on, the people making the decision are named, and the process is described as unfinished. The manuscript's concern is with consequences that attach to position and visibility more than to conduct, and here the reported ground is performance, with no comparison offered of how others in similar roles have been treated. The count of eight managers could point to something above any one manager, but the page gives no account of why, and the entry draws no conclusion from the number alone. An earlier entry on the club's ownership recorded a claim that its value does not depend on results; a report connecting that claim to how managers are judged would be needed before this entry could be read any other way. It is included, briefly, so that a sacking story is not counted as evidence.
